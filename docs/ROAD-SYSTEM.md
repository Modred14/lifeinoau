# Road system

One graph drives everything: the roads are drawn, driven on, walked beside and routed over from the same geometry (`public/data/oau-roadgraph.json`).

## Pipeline (`npm run build:roads`, which runs `tools/oau/build-roads.mjs`)
One command on any OS; it needs python with numpy, scipy, scikit-image and shapely. Which map it builds is the active one (`node tools/oau/use-map.mjs new|legacy`).
0. `tools/oau/dump-merged.mjs` writes the active map plus the game's extras (`withExtras`) to a temporary file, and `tools/oau/roadgraph.py` rasterises it and skeletonises the roads into `tools/oau/roadgraph.raw.json`. (`dump-merged.mjs` was missing from the repository and was recreated; run on the old map it reproduces the committed raw graph, node for node.)
1. `lib3d/roadrefine.js`: straighten (Douglas-Peucker + arcs), prune whiskers/loops/duplicates/fragments, classify (arterial / collector / local). Every change is refused if a building or place would lose road access. For the new map (`data.meta.oldToNew`) three passes are added, because the new map's roads are not hand-cleaned: **3a2** a bend tighter than 7 m is re-fitted with long straight runs, and a cul-de-sac loop becomes a short stub (a road cannot be wider than its bend is round: the mesh folds over); **3c2** the little Y-forks the skeleton makes where a road meets the frame (1 to 10 m, too short for a lane) are cut off; **3d** a dead-end street is joined to the nearest other road when a straight link of at most 140 m keeps 4 m clear of every wall. `EXTRA_ARTERIAL` (an edge id of the old graph) is applied to the old map only.
2. `lib3d/roadalign.js`: hairpins become a straight link (or go), S-bending roads are re-laid as a few long straight runs, side roads are squared to meet a through road at a right angle. Nothing moves a building; each change must keep the road as clear of walls as before.
3. `lib3d/roadshape.js`: contract roundabouts/rings, merge split roads, carry dead ends on to another road or the map edge, put a junction wherever roads cross.
4. `lib3d/highway.js`: the elevated expressway and its ramps. Its waypoints, end extension, interchanges and tails are per map, in `lib3d/mapLayout.js`. A ramp starts `minLen + 40` m before its junction (`HW.ramp.minLen` 365 m, so every ramp climbs the deck under 4 %).
5. `tools/oau/attach-tails.mjs` (idempotent; edits `oau-roadgraph.json` in place and leaves every ground road id alone): `attachTails()` joins the expressway to the road network, and `relocatePiers()` moves any pier that stands in a road.
6. `tools/oau/use-map.mjs --save` stores the result as the active map's own copy.
Running the chain again on the same data changes nothing (checked: the rebuilt `oau-roadgraph.json` is byte-identical).

## The two maps
The old map's expressway was found by searching for a line that cleared every old building by 24 m. That cannot be transformed to the new map: it would cut through its much denser buildings. `tools/oau/search_expressway_routes.py` searches the new map the same way: **straight, 24 m from every building, at least 4.2 km, and over the lake cannot all hold** (4 straight lines clear 24 m; the longest is 4.06 km inside the 480 m ends; none crosses the lake). The new map's layout therefore uses the best straight line, (0, 1260) to (2340, 3240), 55 m from the nearest building, with 600 m ends (4,265 m of deck, 9,018 m with its tails), and does not cross the lake. Its two tails turn the deck round outside the map on to return lines that end at the roads leaving the west rim (R 340 m) and the south rim (R 600 m).
Two interchanges: a half diamond on a local road at s = 950 m, and a full diamond on two collectors at s = 2200 m. The ground "Ile-Ife Expressway" along the south edge of the map is an ordinary road and is not part of the elevated expressway.

## How the expressway joins the roads (`lib3d/highwaytails.js`)
The deck does not end in a barrier. Each end is a **tail**: the straight deck runs on past the map rim, one tangent arc (the radius is per map, in `lib3d/mapLayout.js`; old map west R 130 m, east R 250 m; solved exactly) turns it back on to a return line aimed at a campus road, the last 330 m of that line are a **descent** (3 % average grade, the same smoothed profile as every ramp, `roadgraph.climbFrac`), and at the foot (the **touchdown**) the carriageway carries straight on as ground road: **C1** (the deck's own cross-section, so there is no step), a **taper** node (`taper: true`: the junction polygon narrows the road over the longest gentlest slope), then **C2**, a standard arterial that ends in a junction with the campus road (west: node 51, east: node 28). Both tails can be driven in both directions (`tests/road-network.test.mjs`, 3b). The parapet, median barrier and girder taper to nothing as the deck meets the ground (`highwaymesh.taperK`); nothing stands above the road at the touchdown. Piers are omitted where the deck is below 3.6 m: the foot of a descent is solid fill.

## What is solid (`lib3d/worldsvc.js`, `structuresolid.js`, `furnitureplan.js`, `woodland.js`)
- **Street furniture**: every street light, tree and stop sign is a solid post, from the same plan (`furnitureplan.js`) the meshes use. Each keeps 0.3 m (plus its own radius) off any carriageway or turn patch. They are *soft* for the walking planner only (a `soft` tag shortens the clearance the planner demands), so people are routed along the pavement, but they are as solid as a wall to everything that moves.
- **Woodland trees** (`woodland.js`): the drawn trees and the solid trunks are the same list; a tree hidden because a road runs there is not an obstacle.
- **Ramps and descents**: where the deck is under walking-under height (2.4 m) it is solid fill, so there are walls along both sides and across the low mouth. They are `foot` walls: they stop people only, a car drives along the top and is never stopped by them.
- **Cars** are confined to carriageway and probed against every static solid every 0.5 m however fast they go (`carphysics.MAX_STEP_M`).
- The walking planner's grid extent ignores street furniture and the tails (`outer`), so adding them cannot shift the grid and change borderline routes.

## At load time
- `lib3d/roadgraph.js`: cross-sections (collectors come in a few standard widths), lanes, connectors, routing, surface queries. Junctions get polygons from `lib3d/roadjunction.js`.
- `lib3d/roadjunction.js` (new): one polygon per junction and layer (asphalt, kerb, pavement) with rounded kerb returns. Each road's ribbon starts exactly where the polygon's mouth is, so surfaces meet with no gap or overlap. Roads that meet at a very sharp angle, or would need an arm cut back more than 18 m, are built as a round junction instead (still gapless).
- `lib3d/roadmesh.js`: the surfaces (textured asphalt and paving, kerb stone, shoulders, median with yellow-and-black kerb) and markings (white edge and lane lines, yellow centre lines, stop lines, zebra crossings, lane arrows).
- `lib3d/roadfurniture.js` (new): instanced street lights, pavement trees and stop signs, placed only on pavement/median, clear of walls, crossings and the expressway corridor.

## Tests
`tests/world-obstructions.test.mjs` checks that nothing solid stands on a road, that a car can drive every lane without touching a solid, that people are stopped by posts, trees and the solid parts of ramps while cars are not, and that nothing can be driven through at speed.
`tests/road-network.test.mjs` checks the network, the expressway, junction polygons and mouths, holes in the asphalt and pavement meshes, a car footprint swept along every turn and lane, routing over the whole network, marking clashes and furniture placement.
